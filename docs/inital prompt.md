Build a turn-based adventure game where a player explores a big and rich living world with a simple, but engaging economic system, population system, on-map fighting system, emerging events like trading caravans, bandits, migrants, wildlife, and roaming monsters that sometimes even attack settlements.

I want it to look like a hexagonal map with elevation all done in consistent pixel art, store every asset separately. It needs a few continents and islands, different levels of elevation: from costals at the same level as seas to mountain peaks, vegetation from bushes to sparse trees to deep forests. Hamlets, villages, towns, cities over multiple hexes with specialized buildings: mines, fields, lumber camps, hunter lodges, smelters, smithies, clay pits, etc. Each settlement needs to have a name on the map, population, stockpile of resources, roads leading to nearby settlements, and should belong to a kingdom.

The map should be generated in layers, from the shape of landmasses to vegetation, to resources where they make sense (stone, clay, iron, gold), to settlements connected by roads. Dense forests and seas should be considered resources of wild game and fish. Additional dense forests should spawn roaming rabbits, deer, wolves, and bears (the last two are aggressive and might attack other animals or characters).

Each person in the game should be modeled as a potential character on the map. They should have hit points, action points, age, skills (level of experience in jobs or in using specific weapons), history (where they were born, what they have done, what they have achieved in life), traits, and flaws. We will model small numbers of population, but make every character distinct.The world should have seasons (spring, summer, autumn, winter) affecting the economy system and behavior of wild animals and monsters. For example, wheat should be sown in spring, grow in summer, be harvested in autumn, and people need to survive through winter. Bears should sleep through winter, but sometimes wake up and become super aggressive. For simplicity, we model each season to last over 60 turns. So, a turn is roughly 2 days of real time.

Each settlement should have a home or a few of different densities. For villages, there are village or fisherman homes: these can employ farmers or fishermen and produce vegetables or fish. They can go from 1 to 3 levels of density, each housing up to 6 people. Fisherman houses can only be on a shore, and village houses can be anywhere. Another type is a city home, which goes from 3 to 9 levels of density and cannot produce any food but can house up to 54 people.

Each settlement provides jobs based on the buildings they have. Characters choose jobs based on what is needed in the settlement. Each settlement should have a dream about what they want to do next: for example, a settlement near a deep forest may want to build a hunter lodge or a lumber camp (but not both, because chopping the forest will deplete wildlife). Settlements should be able to evaluate their current and future needs and create a trader to go to nearby settlements to buy necessary goods. For that, a settlement needs to sell something useful first to obtain money to the treasury.

The economy system should include dynamic pricing based on the availability and demand of goods so that traders are motivated to buy where there is an abundance and sell to where it is the highest demand. Settlements should track their needs over time. Demand comes from population needs and plans for development.

Make sure the economic system is balanced altogether so that a location with useful resources, say a home + mine, can grow over time into a prosperous village, and a village may grow into a city in the right conditions.Each character has action points per turn, the base is 4. Movement on a road takes 1AP, going one level of elevation adds +1AP, rough terrain +1AP, dense forest or mountains 2AP. When a character moves to a tile with another character (say a caravan or a rabbit), they should be shown side-by-side so that both are visible. When there is more shown in a circle around the central figure of the character. This way no matter how many characters are on a tile, they are all clickable.

Clicking a wild animal while staying on the same tile is an attack on it. Clicking a caravan is a robbery (but the game will ask if you really want to do it if you still have a good reputation). Cities should spawn guards that will attack you if you have a bad reputation.

Once a user has exhausted their actions, the turn automatically happens, and on the world turn, everyone else acts at the same time, spending their action points on traveling or doing jobs in settlements.

When the HP of a character drops to 0, it either dies or miraculously survives and wakes up in a nearby city. Visiting a city restores HP to max.

Use TypeScript and vote to build this game in a browser, use React for the HUD. You may choose any other suitable libraries and frameworks as you see fit. Please use small files and reuse code elegantly. Avoid putting too much logic in one file.
Take as much time as necessary to complete these tasks thoroughly and refactor as you build. Go the extra mile on making the world internally consistent, evolving, and enjoyable. Finish only when you have a working and playable game.
